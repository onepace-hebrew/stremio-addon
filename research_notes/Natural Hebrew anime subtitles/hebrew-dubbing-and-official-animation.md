# How Israeli dubbing translators and professional subtitlers of animation and anime get natural Hebrew dialogue

Scope: official and professional Hebrew dubbing and subtitling of animation and anime, not One Piece and not fansubs (another researcher covers those). Sources are web articles, academic papers, and four Hebrew subtitle files I pulled from the community mirror wizdom.xyz into the scratchpad (`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-isar-git-onepace-hebrew-stremio/48c368d3-3940-4b66-abac-e65768540f81/scratchpad/dubbing/`). Each file's provenance is stated where I quote it. Text marked **(my judgment)** is mine and has no outside source.

## Q1. Which Hebrew dubs are praised for natural or funny Hebrew, who translated them, and what do those translators say?

### Takeaway
The Hebrew dubs that people remember as funny (Shrek 1 and 2) were done by translator-directors who **domesticated hard**. They swapped American references for Israeli ones, used Israeli slang, and chose the joke that works over the literal line. The older Disney-classic school put **correct, elevated Hebrew** first, and critics later called its translations dated. I found no translator interviews about Ice Age, Madagascar, Kung Fu Panda or Despicable Me, or about any anime dub.

### Cited Findings
- **Shrek 2, Hebrew dub.** Translator and director **Sharon Cohen** of Elrom Studios (אולפני "אלרום") replaced a Lorena Bobbitt joke with «לעשות לו דוד ד'אור שם למטה» (roughly "do a David D'Or on him down there"; D'Or is an Israeli countertenor). Cohen: «כשאני מתרגם אני לא מנסה להתחכם, אלא לא לאבד את רוח המקור, כדי שהצופים ירגישו שהם לא מחמיצים את רוח החוויה» ("When I translate I'm not trying to be clever, just not to lose the spirit of the original, so viewers don't feel they're missing the experience"). He explains he put in an Israeli high-voiced celebrity "instead of the name of someone with a high voice known in the US", and that "the audience laughed for three straight minutes". — [Ynet, «דיבוב עם ביצים», Smadar Shiloni](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-2941962,00.html)
- **Shrek (2001), Hebrew dub vs. Hebrew subtitles.** Rachel Weissbrod (Bar-Ilan) compared the two Hebrew versions:
  - the old language norm "has weakened even in dubbing";
  - domestication is "far more conspicuous in the dubbed version";
  - "The Muffin Man" became the Israeli children's song «אוגה אוגה»;
  - the translator used markedly Israeli slang, e.g. «אחלה דיל» ("great deal", Arabic *akhla* + English *deal*);
  - a remnant of the old norm survives: the vulgar "broad" (for Snow White) became the milder «נערה» ("young girl").

  She concludes that dubbing is target-oriented and subtitling source-oriented. — [Weissbrod 2021, *Linguistica Antverpiensia NS–TTS* 6](https://doi.org/10.52034/lanstts.v6i.177)
- **Shrek trilogy, Hebrew DVD versions.** An MA thesis (נירה סתו לב-רן, Bar-Ilan, Department of Translation, 2011):
  - The dubbing **kept more wordplay** than the subtitles did.
  - In all three films, the dub was **based on a subtitle translation prepared first**.
  - Dubbed speech that sounds artificial is called «דיבובית» ("dubbese"). It appears when translators "give up idiomaticity in favour of fidelity to the source".
  - Citing DeMarco, the thesis says subtitlers tend to be more literal than dubbing translators.

  — [Lev-Ran thesis, doczz mirror](https://doczz.net/doc/6874649/%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%94-%D7%95%D7%94%D7%93%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%92%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99-%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9C%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%91%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%93%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%91-%D7%91%D7%A1%D7%A8%D7%98%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9E)
- **Disney classics.** **Shprira Zakai** (שפרירה זכאי) translated The Lion King, Aladdin and The Little Mermaid (films and songs), as well as Sesame Street and many cartoon theme songs, and directed voice actors. Her interview covers "insisting on correct, elevated Hebrew (עברית תקינה וגבוהה) in content aimed mainly at children". The page gives no concrete line examples. — [Leshoniada podcast page, 2019](https://leshoniada.co.il/2019/09/08/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A7-14-%D7%9B%D7%A9%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%91%D7%94-%D7%A4%D7%92%D7%A9-%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%93%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%97%D7%94-%D7%A2%D7%9D-%D7%A9%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%99/)
- **A critic on the older dubs.** Yair Raveh, a film critic and parent, found early-2000s Hebrew dub translations "very dated" («מיושן מאוד») and unfunny for children. He was "broken" by The Lion King 3, where Timon's "Skylight!" was dubbed «אור מהשמים!» ("light from the heavens!"). He says later generations of dubbers replaced the old guard, translations were updated, and "the jokes started being funny in Hebrew too". — [Cinemascope, Yair Raveh, 2014](https://cinemascope.co.il/archives/17641)
- **Disney's own brief.** Rick Dempsey, head of Disney Character Voice: "There's always the traditional way of saying things, which would be a straight translation, but we don't talk that way... the slang that is used in the English version gets translated in its slang in the international versions." — [D23, "Making Movie Magic in Any Language"](https://d23.com/making-movie-magic-in-any-language/)
- **Pokémon (anime), Hebrew dub.**
  - Dubbed at **NLS Recording Studios** (אולפני נ.ל.ס) from 1999 to 2006/7 for ערוץ הילדים.
  - Dubbing director: **Yafa Gabai** (יפה גבאי); Maya Bar Shalom directed *Pokémon Chronicles*.
  - Later seasons moved to Jetix / Disney Israel.

  No translator credit was found. — [Hebrew Wikipedia mirror, «פוקימון (סדרת טלוויזיה)»](https://www.classicistranieri.com/he/%D7%A4/%D7%95/%D7%A7/%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9F_%28%D7%A1%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%AA_%D7%98%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%96%D7%99%D7%94%29.html)
- *Pokémon the Movie: I Choose You!* was aired dubbed on ערוץ הילדים in 2018, and the title keeps the catchphrase «אני בוחר בך!» ("I choose you!"). — [he.wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%94%D7%A1%D7%A8%D7%98:_%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%91%D7%95%D7%97%D7%A8_%D7%91%D7%9A!)
- **Fan reaction to voice changes.** Fans on a 2009 forum judged recasts by whether the new voice kept the character. They praised a replacement who "made a voice almost identical" and panned one who imitated the American dub. — [Tapuz forum](https://www.tapuz.co.il/threads/%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9F.11436197/page-2)

### Inferences
- **(my judgment)** The Hebrew versions that worked share one choice: the translator wrote the line an Israeli would say in that situation, not the English line reworded.
- **(my judgment)** Shrek's pop-culture swaps (David D'Or, Uga Uga) work because Shrek parodies real-world pop culture. One Piece is a closed fictional world, so the transferable lesson is **idiom-level** localization, not swapping in Israeli celebrities or brand names.
- **(my judgment)** Lev-Ran's finding that the Shrek dub was built from a subtitle draft fits our case: a written-first draft drifts toward fidelity and «דיבובית». A "would a dubbing actor say this aloud?" check pulls it back.

### Gaps
- I found no interviews with the Hebrew translators or directors of Toy Story, Ice Age, Madagascar, Kung Fu Panda or Despicable Me. Search turned up only casting news.
- I found no source giving the exact Hebrew wording of the Team Rocket motto, Digimon "digivolve", or other anime-dub catchphrases.
- I could not confirm which Hebrew anime dubs exist beyond Pokémon: whether Digimon, Beyblade, Yu-Gi-Oh! or Naruto were dubbed, and by whom.
- The Zakai interview is audio only. The web page has no line-level content.

## Q2. Known register debates: normative "correct" Hebrew vs. street Hebrew, IBA era vs. today; lines that were praised or ridiculed

### Takeaway
Israeli screen translation went through a documented norm shift. Under the **Israel Broadcasting Authority / Channel 1**, subtitles used elevated register:
- no slang;
- vulgarity put in quotation marks;
- coined Hebrew words in place of known loanwords;
- language editors correcting deviations.

Today that norm has "almost vanished" in subtitles and weakened even in children's dubbing. The mechanical tricks of that era survive and are exactly what makes text read as bookish, e.g. אנו for אנחנו to save characters, and לעזאזל or ארור as all-purpose swears.

### Cited Findings
- **The IBA-era norm, as Weissbrod summarizes it (citing Nir 1984):**
  - "The register was often elevated compared with the original."
  - "Slang and vulgar phrases were avoided, especially in translations for children."
  - "When slang and vulgarisms did find their way into the translation, they were put in quotation marks."
  - "Foreign borrowed words was shunned... the captions often contained newly coined Hebrew words with which the public was not acquainted."
  - Deviations "were corrected by language editors".
  - Her extreme case: a 1992 Channel 1 *Othello* used Aramaic «במטותא» for "please" and «צפרא טבא» for "good morning". Commercial channels did not do this.
  - Today: "The norm described by Nir (1984) has almost vanished."

  — [Weissbrod 2021](https://doi.org/10.52034/lanstts.v6i.177)
- **A Channel 1 subtitler on craft**, in the Academy of the Hebrew Language's journal *לשוננו לעם* (vol. 49/2, pp. 85–89; author name garbled in the extract, not verified):
  - «הכתוביות מתרגמות דו-שיח חי, ולכן עליהן להיות מנוסחות בסגנון הלשון החיה» ("Subtitles translate live dialogue, so they must be phrased in the style of the living language"). Errors here are "common mainly in translating slang".
  - Fillers are not translated, and you don't add words for emotions the speaker already conveys through voice.
  - Children's subtitles need "easier Hebrew" and are cut down further.
  - Wordplay should be recreated in Hebrew when possible. Example: «מטעם ההנהלה, ולפי הריהוט כאן יש להם טעם» ("On behalf of the management, and judging by the furniture, they have taste"), a pun on טעם (both "behalf" in מטעם and "taste").
  - The same article lists abbreviations (ע"י, וכו') as condensation tools.

  — [לשוננו לעם PDF, hebrew-academy.org.il](https://hebrew-academy.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/lla49-2-p.85-89.pdf) (text read via search extract; direct fetch returned 403)
- **A current professional subtitler.** **Yifat Shomer** has subtitled Homeland, The Simpsons and South Park and is house translator at Transtitle («טרנסטייטל»).
  - The goal: «שהתרגום יהיה שקוף, שהצופה יראה את הפרק ולא יקלוט אפילו שהוא קורא תרגום» ("the translation should be transparent; the viewer shouldn't even notice he's reading a translation").
  - «הטעות הכי נפוצה של מתרגמים היא תרגום מילולי... אם יש ביטוי באנגלית, צריך להתאים לו את המקבילה שלו בעברית ולא לתרגם אחד לאחד» ("The most common mistake is literal translation... for an English expression, find its Hebrew counterpart, don't translate one-to-one").
  - Children's series differ from adult thrillers "in the Hebrew used, in word choice suited to each age".
  - She also names a condensation trick: «במקום לכתוב 'אנחנו' כותבים 'אנו'» ("instead of אנחנו we write אנו").

  — [Makor Rishon, Dotan Malach, 2019](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/culture/article/259707) (read via search extract; direct fetch returned 403)
- **The opposite school.** Zakai's stated emphasis on «עברית תקינה וגבוהה» for children. — [Leshoniada](https://leshoniada.co.il/2019/09/08/%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A7-14-%D7%9B%D7%A9%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%91%D7%94-%D7%A4%D7%92%D7%A9-%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%93%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%97%D7%94-%D7%A2%D7%9D-%D7%A9%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%99/)
- **Ridiculed dated dub line:** «אור מהשמים!» for "Skylight!" in The Lion King 3 (see Q1). — [Cinemascope](https://cinemascope.co.il/archives/17641)
- **Register update shown line by line (literary, but the same craft).** Translator **Gili Bar-Hillel**, on her new Hebrew *Catcher in the Rye* (interviewed by Ruvik Rosenthal), set beside the older translation (Daniel/Yavin):

  | English | Older translation | Bar-Hillel |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | royal pain in the ass | «כאב תחת מלכותי» | «קוץ בתחת» |
  | fouled that up | «חירבנתי» | «פישלתי» |
  | bastard | «ממזר» | «מניאק» |
  | moron | «מטומטם» | «דפקט / פראייר» |
  | only kidding | «מתחתי אותו» | «עובד עליו» |
  | boy! | «שמעו!» | «שיואו» |
  | goddam it | «לכל הרוחות» | (much stronger Israeli curse) |
  | crap | «טינופת» | «חרטוט / ג'יפה» |
  | fart | «נאד» | «פלוץ» |
  | hot shot guy | «שוויצר» | «חתיך» |
  | I don't feel like | — | «לא בא לי» |

  More from the interview:
  - She dropped «פושטקים» because it «התיישנה» ("has gone stale").
  - She fought to keep «סבבה» and «אחלה» so that not everything comes out «נפלא».
  - She quotes Shlonsky: «מתרגם הוא שחקן» ("a translator is an actor").
  - Her aim: «למצוא את השפה שתידמה לאותנטית ותידמה לטבעית» ("to find language that seems authentic and natural").

  — [Ruvik Rosenthal, «החתיך, המניאק והחרטטן», 2023](https://www.ruvik.co.il/%D7%94%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99/2023/31323.aspx)
- **Subtitlers' forum on curses** (Qsubs, a Hebrew subtitling community, so fan-adjacent and not official). The consensus reply: match the character's bluntness and don't launder it — «אין אף אחד שמקלל במילים מכובסות כמו 'ישבן'» ("nobody curses with laundered words like 'buttocks'"). Because Hebrew has fewer curse slots, "the effort is how to add, not how to subtract". — [Qsubs forum, 2014](http://www.qsubs.net/index.php?showtopic=62108)
- **Hebrew localization at Google (UX writing, but the same register problem).** Kinneret Yifrah: "a translation may be correct in terms of grammar and syntax, but that doesn't make it natural"; disconnect from the English words and "create the sentence from scratch, as if it was written in Hebrew in the first place". Her four-part slang test:
  1. **Durability** — has it lasted?
  2. **Prevalence** — known to all ages and backgrounds?
  3. **Audience** — fits who is reading?
  4. **Dosage** — "slang is a spice, not the main course".

  — [Medium, Kinneret Yifrah, 2018](https://medium.com/@Kinneret/a-glimpse-into-google-israels-great-language-and-localization-update-project-9fa83f4489c9)

### Inferences
- **(my judgment)** The bookish feel our native reader flags is the IBA norm leaking back in. Elevated vocabulary (זנחתי, ציווה, אסיר תודה) and literary set phrases (מחזיר ימים, X רודף X) are grammatical and "correct", which is why a model trained on edited written Hebrew produces them. The fix is the Bar-Hillel move: swap to the word Israelis actually use (ויתרתי, אמר, מודה לך / תודה).
- **(my judgment)** Shomer's «אנו» trick is a warning sign for us. Professional condensation habits (אנו, ע"י, ובכן, הנך) save space but raise the register. Condense by **dropping words** (fillers, pronouns, redundant emotion words), not by switching to a higher-register synonym.

### Gaps
- I found no published article that lists "translationese" stock phrases in Hebrew subtitles (e.g. a Ruvik Rosenthal column on «תרגומית»), even though the phenomenon is widely known. Searches returned forum chatter only.
- The Netflix **Hebrew Timed Text Style Guide** was not retrieved. The old partnerhelp URL returns 404, and the backlothelp.netflix.com listing I found did not include Hebrew. It may hold Netflix's official register guidance for Hebrew.

## Q3. Which anime and animated series have official Hebrew subtitles or dubs, and how does official Hebrew render shonen-type beats?

### Takeaway
On Netflix Israel, official Hebrew **subtitles** exist mainly for Netflix originals and some licensed films. Examples: Cyberpunk: Edgerunners, Pluto, Blue Eye Samurai, Arcane, the Ghibli films, Rick and Morty. Hebrew **dubs** are essentially limited to children's titles. The official subtitles get action beats right with short, everyday imperatives and verb-based slang. They still keep English calques (מכוער for "ugly", לחצנו יד for "shook on it") and a default «לעזאזל». So the official subs are a decent register model, but the calques show they are not a gold standard.

### Cited Findings
- **Availability.**
  - Hebrew subtitles on Netflix anime exist "for some titles, mainly Netflix originals like Cyberpunk: Edgerunners, Pluto and Blue Eye Samurai"; Hebrew dubbing "almost doesn't exist for adult anime". — [GeekGuild, Sept 2026](https://geekguild.co.il/articles/best-anime-netflix-2026)
  - Netflix IL lists Hebrew subtitles for *Spirited Away* («המסע המופלא») — [Netflix IL](https://www.netflix.com/il/title/60023642) — and for *Rick and Morty* — [Netflix IL-en](https://www.netflix.com/il-en/title/80014749).
  - A fan blog catalogued Netflix anime with Hebrew subtitles: Little Witch Academia, Fate/Apocrypha, Kabaneri, Godzilla trilogy, The Tale of Princess Kaguya, A Whisker Away, Drifting Dragons. For one film it notes the Netflix subtitles "were improved and edited by WiseFix", which suggests fans saw room to improve. — [netflixhebrewanime2000 blog](https://netflixhebrewanime2000.wordpress.com/category/%D7%90%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%92%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%A1/)
  - A MyAnimeList list of anime "with official or fan-made Hebrew subtitles" includes Kimetsu no Yaiba, Sakamoto Days, Vinland Saga, Naruto and Pokémon, but does not separate official from fan. — [MAL stack 89309](https://myanimelist.net/stacks/89309)
- **Netflix in general.** When subtitling *Chelsea*, Netflix tested over 5,000 linguists on how they handle "vulgarities, slang... and idioms such as 'she's as tough as a $2 steak'". In other words, idiom transfer is a hiring criterion. — [Netflix, 2016](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/delivering-a-global-talk-show-the-innovation-behind-chelsea)

**Sample lines from official-release subtitles.** Provenance:

- *Blue Eye Samurai* S01E01, end credit «תרגום כתוביות: ג'ניה ליטבאק».

**Arcane (Netflix), what works:**
- «כן, רק אם נפשל.» / «אז אל תפשלו.» — "Yeah, only if we screw up." / "Then don't screw up." Verb slang פישל, echoed back.
- «השטח פנוי.» — "Coast is clear." Uses the Hebrew equivalent idiom instead of a literal "coast".
- «פשוט תעזבו את זה! קדימה!» — "Just drop it! Come on!"
- «קדימה, עוף מפה!» — "Go on, get out of here!"
- «אני חוטף בפנים ולה פשוט מוותרים?» — "I take one in the face and she just gets let off?"
- «היא מנחסת כל עבודה!» — "She jinxes every job!" Jinx is rendered with the slang verb נחס.
- «חתיכת…» — "You piece of…" Insult cut off.
- Action barks, short and imperative: «זהירות!», «תחזיקו חזק!», «עצרו מייד!», «מהר יותר!», «מכאן!» ("Watch out!", "Hold on tight!", "Stop right there!", "Faster!", "This way!").

**Arcane (Netflix), calques that slipped through the official sub:**
- «אין סיבה שזה יהיה מכוער.» — "No reason this has to get ugly." The same non-physical «מכוער» calque our native reader flagged.
- «לחצנו על זה יד.» — "We shook on it." Literal.
- «אפשר למלא ספרייה ארורה בדברים שלא עשית.» — "You could fill a damn library with what you didn't do." «ארורה» as an all-purpose "damn".
- «ובכן, אני מניח שזה מסכם את העניין שלנו.» — "Well, I guess that settles our business." Starts with «ובכן».
- «מה לעזאזל קרה שם?» — "What the hell happened there?"

**Blue Eye Samurai (Netflix), one cast with several distinct voices:**
- Street and rough:
  - «אף אחד לא מתעסק עם האצ'י!» — "Nobody messes with Hachi!"
  - «לך תזדיין.» — "Fuck off."
  - «כפריות רזות שכמותכן. תאכלו!» — "You skinny peasant girls. Eat!" Uses the Hebrew insult pattern «X שכמותך/ן».
  - «פשוט תקפוץ.» — "Just jump."
  - «הבא בתור!» — "Next!"
- Formal swordsman:
  - «אתה לא ראוי ללהב שלי.» — "You're not worthy of my blade."
  - «עליי לסרב.» — "I must decline."
- Ritual and prayer:
  - «אנא הענק לי את הכוח להרוג אותם.» — "Please grant me the strength to kill them." «אנא» and the classical imperative «הענק».
  - «אני נכנעת לרצונך בלב שלם.» — a princess: "I submit to your will wholeheartedly."
- Polite request: «אני אהיה אסיר תודה.» — "I'd be grateful."
- Honorific: «אקמי־סאמה» — Netflix keeps "-sama" as a loan. This differs from our house «אדון/גברת» convention.

**Spirited Away (official Hebrew subtitles credited to שלמה ליברמן):**
- Dignified speaker (Haku), classical imperative:
  - «זכרי, אני חבר שלך.» — "Remember, I'm your friend."
  - «בלי פטפוטי סרק.» — "No idle chatter."
  - «תקראי לי אדון האקו.» — "Call me Master Haku." This is the same «אדון + name» pattern as our house -san rule.
- Elder (Kamaji): «הנח לזה.» — "Leave it."
- Everyday speakers (Lin, Yubaba), future tense used as the imperative:
  - «תפסיקי לבהות.» — "Stop staring."
  - «תפסיקי להתבטל.» — "Stop dawdling."
  - «מה דעתך על "כן, גברתי" או "תודה"?» — "How about 'Yes, ma'am' or 'Thank you'?"
- Hebrew intensifier idiom: «היא מסריחה עד לב שמיים.» — "She stinks to high heaven." Uses the Hebrew «עד לב שמיים» rather than a calque.
- Short exclamations: «איזו צרה!» ("What a nuisance!"), «אתה תשלם על זה.» ("You'll pay for this.")

### Inferences
- **(my judgment)** Official subs mark **character voice through imperative form**:
  - classical imperative («זכרי», «הנח», «הענק») plus «אנא» or «עליי ל» for dignified, old or ritual speakers;
  - future-as-imperative («תפסיקי», «תעזבו», «תקפוץ») for everyone else.

  This maps directly onto our cast: Whitebeard, Sengoku and Rayleigh can carry an occasional classical imperative; Luffy, the crew and the brawlers never should.
- **(my judgment)** The calques in the Arcane sub (מכוער, לחצנו יד, ארורה, ובכן) are the same kind of error as our flagged lines. So copying official subs is not enough; we need an explicit calque list.
- **(my judgment)** «אסיר תודה» does occur in official subs, but in a conditional, formal request («אהיה אסיר תודה»). That supports our rule: a plain "thank you" becomes «תודה» or «אני מודה לך», never «אני אסיר תודה לך».

### Gaps
- I could not get a readable official Hebrew sub for a true **shonen** title. Demon Slayer, JJK, Sakamoto Days and Dan Da Dan were not on Wizdom, and the Edgerunners file was corrupted mojibake. Netflix shonen subs (Sakamoto Days, Demon Slayer if licensed in IL) remain the best untapped reference.
- I don't know whether Disney+ IL, yes, HOT or Kan carry any anime with Hebrew subtitles. I found nothing citable.
- I did not compare Hebrew dub scripts to their subtitle tracks for any anime, because no Hebrew-dubbed anime script was available.

## Q4. Concrete transferable techniques, each with a real example

### Takeaway
Seven techniques recur across professionals:
1. replace the English idiom with the **Hebrew idiom that does the same job**;
2. use **verb-based spoken slang**;
3. condense by **dropping**, not by elevating;
4. **recreate** wordplay instead of explaining it;
5. use **morphology** to give each character a voice;
6. match **insult strength** to the character;
7. **update dated words** while avoiding both old literary words and trendy slang.

### Cited Findings
- **Idiom for idiom.** Shomer: "find the Hebrew counterpart, don't translate one-to-one" ([Makor Rishon](https://www.makorrishon.co.il/culture/article/259707)). Examples:
  - Arcane: «השטח פנוי» for "coast is clear" (sample above).
  - Spirited Away: «מסריחה עד לב שמיים» for "stinks to high heaven" (sample above).
  - Bar-Hillel: «קוץ בתחת» for "royal pain in the ass", replacing the older calque «כאב תחת מלכותי» ([Ruvik](https://www.ruvik.co.il/%D7%94%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99/2023/31323.aspx)).
- **Cultural substitution in dubbing.**
  - Muffin Man → «אוגה אוגה»; slang «אחלה דיל» ([Weissbrod](https://doi.org/10.52034/lanstts.v6i.177)).
  - Bobbitt → David D'Or ([Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-2941962,00.html)).
- **Verb-based spoken slang over noun phrases.**
  - «פישלתי» over «חירבנתי» ([Ruvik](https://www.ruvik.co.il/%D7%94%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99/2023/31323.aspx)).
  - Arcane: «נפשל», «מנחסת», «חוטף בפנים»; Blue Eye Samurai: «מתעסק עם» (samples above).
- **Don't translate fillers or emotion already in the voice; children get easier Hebrew.** — [לשוננו לעם](https://hebrew-academy.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/lla49-2-p.85-89.pdf)
- **Recreate wordplay in Hebrew:** «מטעם ההנהלה... יש להם טעם». — [לשוננו לעם](https://hebrew-academy.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/lla49-2-p.85-89.pdf)
- **Dubbing keeps more wordplay than subtitling.** Subtitlers are more literal (DeMarco, cited by Lev-Ran). — [Lev-Ran 2011](https://doczz.net/doc/6874649/%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%94-%D7%95%D7%94%D7%93%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%92%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99-%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9C%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%91%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%93%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%91-%D7%91%D7%A1%D7%A8%D7%98%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9E)
- **Character voice.**
  - A Spanish dubbing translator: "each character has their own way of speaking and a particular vocabulary... a register and distinctive touch you have to respect" ([Asymptote, 2019](https://www.asymptotejournal.com/blog/2019/09/11/flowing-speech-on-the-complexities-of-audiovisual-translation/)).
  - Bar-Hillel keeps an old-fashioned word, «גריפה», for an elderly character ([Ruvik](https://www.ruvik.co.il/%D7%94%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99/2023/31323.aspx)).
- **Insults at the character's level, not laundered.** — [Qsubs](http://www.qsubs.net/index.php?showtopic=62108); Blue Eye Samurai «לך תזדיין», «X שכמותכן» (sample above).
- **Slang dosage and staying power.** — [Yifrah](https://medium.com/@Kinneret/a-glimpse-into-google-israels-great-language-and-localization-update-project-9fa83f4489c9); «פושטקים» dropped as stale ([Ruvik](https://www.ruvik.co.il/%D7%94%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2%D7%99/2023/31323.aspx)).
- **The colloquial version carries the humor; the standard-language version deflates it.** In a comparable diglossic case (Arabic Monsters, Inc.: Egyptian colloquial dub vs. Modern Standard Arabic redub), "resorting to colloquialism... provided a functionally adequate, nuanced leeway... to capture the situational humour", while the standard variety "significantly deflated and sacrificed verbal humour". — [Yahiaoui 2022, EJHR](https://europeanjournalofhumour.org/ejhr/article/view/681)

### Inferences
- **(my judgment)** The most useful test for our pipeline is a **"dub test"**: would a Hebrew voice actor say this line aloud in the scene? Dubbing translators can't hide behind written register. Weissbrod and Lev-Ran both show dubbing is the more target-oriented, more idiomatic mode.

### Gaps
- No source gives Hebrew examples of compensation (moving a lost joke to another line) in animation.
- No source documents pronoun-dropping in Hebrew subtitles. It is consistent with the "drop, don't elevate" principle, but that link is my extrapolation.

## Actionable rules for our translator

Ranked by expected impact on the "bookish / translated" complaint. Each rule gives the **pattern**, **why it's unnatural**, **the natural alternative(s)**, and **an example**. Suggested rewrites of our own lines are **(my judgment)** unless a source is named.

### 1. Calqued English collocations and metaphors → the Hebrew idiom that does the same job
- **Why unnatural:** every word is real Hebrew, but only the English explains the pairing. Shomer calls one-to-one translation "the most common mistake". Even Netflix's Arcane sub slips («מכוער», «לחצנו יד»).
- **Natural alternatives:**

  | English idea | Calque | Natural Hebrew |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | get ugly / ugly side effects | «מכוער» | «יתלכלך», «ייגמר רע», «קשות» |
  | shook on it | «לחצנו יד» | «סגרנו», «סיכמנו» |
  | a cornered rat is scary | «עכבר לכוד הוא דבר מפחיד» | «גם עכבר בפינה יכול לנשוך» |
  | coast is clear (Arcane) | — | «השטח פנוי» |
  | stinks to high heaven (Spirited Away) | — | «מסריחה עד לב שמיים» |

- **Example from our files:** «הבהלה הזאת ממש נוחה לנו.» → «הבלגן הזה משחק לטובתנו.» ("This chaos plays in our favour.")

### 2. "As expected of X" / "no less than you'd expect of X" → «לא סתם…», «ברור ש…», «איך לא», «מה ציפית»
- **Why unnatural:** this is the Japanese *sasuga* relayed through English. «כצפוי מ…» and «לא פחות ממה שאפשר לצפות מ…» are newspaper or academic Hebrew. No source covers this pattern; the rule is my judgment, built on Shomer's idiom-for-idiom principle.
- **Examples:**
  - «כצפוי מכל כוחות הצי.» → «לא סתם זה הצי.» ("It's not for nothing they're the Marines.") / «מה חשבת, זה הצי.» ("What did you expect, it's the Marines.")
  - «לא פחות ממה שאפשר לצפות ממישהו שהפליג עם מלך הפיראטים!» → «לא סתם הוא הפליג עם מלך הפיראטים!» ("Not for nothing he sailed with the Pirate King!")

### 3. High-register single words where spoken Hebrew has a plain word → use the plain word (the Bar-Hillel move)
- **Why unnatural:** this is the IBA-era norm (elevated register, language editors). It is correct Hebrew that nobody says. Bar-Hillel: «חירבנתי→פישלתי», «מטומטם→דפקט», «שמעו!→שיואו», «I don't feel like → לא בא לי».
- **Natural alternatives:**
  - «זנחתי» → «ויתרתי»
  - «ציווה» → «אמר» / «נתן הוראה»
  - «אני אסיר תודה לך» → «אני מודה לך» / «תודה»
  - «ובכן,» → «טוב,» / «אז,» / omit
  - «הבה» → «בואו»
  - «אנא» → «בבקשה» (keep «אנא» only for prayer or ritual, as in Blue Eye Samurai)
- **Example:** «אבא ציווה לשמור על האח של אייס בחיים!» → «אבא אמר לשמור על האח של אייס בחיים!» ("Pops said to keep Ace's brother alive!")

### 4. Literary set phrases and journalistic constructions → spoken equivalents
- **Why unnatural:** «X רודף X», «מחזיר ימים» and «הרגש סוחב אתכם» are written Hebrew. Raveh's example of the dated, unfunny register is «אור מהשמים!».
- **Example:** «גילוי רודף גילוי, וכולם מטלטלים.» → «גילוי אחרי גילוי, וכולם בהלם.» ("Revelation after revelation, and everyone's in shock.") Note that «מטלטלים» is also the wrong form; it should be «מטולטלים».

### 5. Fixed idioms must be letter-exact; otherwise don't use them
- **Why unnatural:** a fixed expression with one changed word reads as a mistake, not a style. Bar-Hillel's whole method rests on choosing the exact current expression.
- **Example:** «שליט הגיהנום כבר נושם בעורף.» → «שליט הגיהנום כבר נושף בעורף.» ("The ruler of hell is already breathing down our necks.")
- **Check:** verify every idiom against a dictionary (e.g. Morfix or Rav-Milim) before using it.

### 6. Give each character a voice through morphology, not through vocabulary for everyone
- **Why it matters:** official subs (Spirited Away, Blue Eye Samurai) separate dignified speakers («זכרי», «הנח», «הענק», «עליי לסרב», «אינך ראוי») from everyday speakers («תפסיקי», «תעזבו», «תקפוץ», «עוף מפה»). Flattening everyone to the high set is what reads as bookish.
- **Our cast:**
  - Formal old warriors (Whitebeard, Sengoku, Rayleigh) may use the classical imperative and «עליי ל…» occasionally.
  - Luffy, the crew and the brawlers never do. They use the future as imperative, «יאללה», «חבר'ה», «קדימה».
- **Example:** Haku's «זכרי, אני חבר שלך.» ("Remember, I'm your friend") vs Lin's «תפסיקי לבהות.» ("Stop staring").

### 7. Battle cries, barks and taunts → short spoken imperatives or exclamations, Hebrew word order
- **Why unnatural:** English "Get ready for me, world!" rendered word for word («תתכונן לקראתי, עולם!») keeps English syntax and a formal preposition.
- **Natural pattern:** official Netflix barks are «זהירות!», «תחזיקו חזק!», «עצרו מייד!», «הבא בתור!», «פנו דרך!», «קדימה!».
- **Example:** «תתכונן לקראתי, עולם!» → «עולם, תתכונן! אני בא!» ("World, get ready! Here I come!")

### 8. Insults: use Hebrew insult patterns at the character's real strength
- **Why it matters:** Qsubs consensus: "nobody curses with laundered words". IBA-era euphemisms («לכל הרוחות», «ממזר», «טינופת») sound like an old book.
- **Patterns:**
  - «חתיכת X» ("you piece of X")
  - «X שכמותך» ("you X", as in «כפריות רזות שכמותכן»)
  - cut-off «חתיכת…»
  - «יא X» ("you X!")
  - «מניאק», «פראייר», «דפקט» (Bar-Hillel's replacements for older words)
- **For a shonen tone:** keep profanity mild unless the English is strong **(my judgment)**.
- **Avoid as defaults:** reflexive «לעזאזל» and «ארור/ה» for every "damn" or "hell" (seen even in Arcane).
- **Illustrative example (invented, not from our files):** «הו, לעזאזל, ארור תהיה!» → «אוף, חתיכת…!» ("Ugh, you piece of…!")

### 9. Condense by dropping, never by elevating
- **Why:** Shomer's trick («אנו» for «אנחנו») and Channel 1's abbreviations save characters but raise the register.
- **Natural approach** (per the לשוננו לעם article):
  - drop fillers and redundant emotion words;
  - drop subject pronouns where the verb ending carries person;
  - drop "you know", "I mean" and similar fillers.
- **Illustrative example (invented, not from our files):** «אנו נלחמים בשבילך» → «נלחמים בשבילך» ("We're fighting for you"). For our 44-character line limit, cut before you upgrade.

### 10. Thanks and gratitude: plain by default
- **Pattern:** «תודה» / «תודה רבה» / «אני מודה לך» are the default. Keep «אהיה אסיר תודה (אם…)» only for formal conditional requests, which is how Blue Eye Samurai uses it.
- **Example:** «אני אסיר תודה לך» → «אני מודה לך».

### 11. Slang: durable and widely known, a pinch per scene
- **Why:** Yifrah's test (durability, prevalence, audience, dosage). Bar-Hillel dropped stale «פושטקים» but kept «סבבה» and «אחלה».
- **Example:** «יצא לנו מזל» → «שיחק לנו המזל» (an established idiom, not trendy slang).
- **Avoid:** current TikTok slang that will date.

### 12. Passive or "going to be + participle" → active Israeli construction with an impersonal plural
- **Why:** Hebrew avoids periphrastic passives in speech; the impersonal plural («הולכים להוציא») is the spoken equivalent. This is a general Hebrew fact, not drawn from the sources above.
- **Example:** «אייס הולך להיות מוצא להורג» → «הולכים להוציא את אייס להורג».

**Detection hints for the ~340 episodes (my judgment, a starting list):** grep dialogue for:
- «ובכן», «הבה», «אנא», «אנו», «הנך», «אינני», «ציווה», «זנח», «אסיר תודה» (when not followed by «אם»);
- «כצפוי», «ממה שאפשר לצפות», «מכוער/ה/ים» (non-physical), «לחצנו יד», «ארור», «לכל הרוחות», «X רודף X»;
- known idioms with a wrong word (a frozen-idiom whitelist: «נושף בעורף», «שיחק לנו המזל», …);
- classical imperatives («זכור/זכרי», «הנח», «שמע») in lines spoken by non-formal characters.

Each hit needs a human or model read in context; none of these phrases is banned outright (cf. house doc §1.5).
