NATIVE REVIEW — required before `build` (the build refuses without it)

Why: the translator's own un-calque check runs with the English open, and the
anchored ear passes Hebrew that only makes sense if you remember the English.
A reader who never sees the English catches what the writer cannot.

1. node scripts/build-onepace-episode.js review-input <ID> > <scratch>/<ID>.he.txt
2. Spawn TWO fresh agents in parallel (not the translator, no English, no
   cues.json, no notes) with the prompt below, pointing at that file. Take the
   union of their flags: one reader misses a lot (see calibration).
   Calibration used general-purpose agents on Opus 5.5; recheck the numbers
   if the reviewer model changes.
3. With the English open, decide each flag: fix it in <ID>.he.json, or keep it
   with a reason (voice, house style, a reader false alarm).
4. Changed lines get ONE more blind read by a fresh agent (same prompt, only
   the changed lines plus 3 neighbours each side). Fix what it flags; that
   round is final, no third one.
5. Write <ID>.native-review.txt:
     line 1:  sha256 <shasum -a 256 of the FINAL <ID>.he.json>
     then:    one line per flag:  <i> | fixed|kept | <reason>
6. node scripts/build-onepace-episode.js build <ID>

Calibration (2026-10-18, IM_7, record in IM_7.native-review.txt):
- Round 1, one reader, full episode: 32 flags, 28 real.
- Round 2, fresh reader on 193 lines around the fixes: 14 flags, all real,
  12 of them on lines round 1 had passed. Hence two readers in step 2.
- Human gold (EL04), one reader: 26 of 614 flagged. Some of those are lines
  that learnings §1.5 calls good (בול פגיעה, בנאדם); the user hasn't ruled on
  that yet.
A reader always finds something; that is expected. Don't loop until zero.
Human-translated episodes (folders with a " He.ass") are NEVER reviewed for
fixes or edited. Only read them, as calibration.

---- PROMPT (send verbatim, replace <FILE>) ----

You are a native Israeli Hebrew speaker watching an anime (One Piece) with Hebrew subtitles. Read the file <FILE>. Each line is `<number>\t<subtitle line>`, in episode order. Read all of it, in order, so you have the scene context.

Your job: list every line that a native Hebrew speaker would NOT say out loud in that situation. You're looking for lines that are grammatical and understandable but odd: unnatural phrasing, wrong collocation, awkward word order, a wording that only makes sense if you imagine some other language behind it.

Do NOT flag:
- Spelling conventions (הכל, האלו, מידי, דיי, זו), these are house style.
- Character quirks: one character refers to himself as ווי instead of אני; that is intentional.
- Gender/number agreement, names, or punctuation, unless the phrasing itself is odd.
- Anime-style shouting, short exclamations, or theatrical villain speech that is natural for the genre.

Be strict but honest: flag only lines you are fairly confident a native speaker would find odd. Do not pad the list.

Do not read any other files. Do not look anything up. Do not edit anything.

Final answer: one line per flagged item, exactly `<number> | <the line> | <short reason in English>`, then a last line `TOTAL: <count>`.
